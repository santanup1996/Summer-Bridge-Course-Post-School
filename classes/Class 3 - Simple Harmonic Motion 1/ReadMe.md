# Class 3 — Simple Harmonic Motion (1)


[![Video Thumbnail](https://img.youtube.com/vi/CqXPBicM0m8/0.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CqXPBicM0m8)


## Overview
This class introduces the basic ideas of periodic motion, oscillation, and simple harmonic motion (SHM). The main goal is to connect the physical mechanism of oscillation with its mathematical description.

The class moves from:
- what periodic and oscillatory motion mean,
- to the restoring-force picture,
- to the equation of motion of SHM,
- to the general sinusoidal solution,
- and finally to the relations among displacement, velocity, and acceleration.

## Main topics
- Periodic motion and oscillation
- Period, frequency, and amplitude
- Equilibrium position and restoring force
- Hooke’s law: \(F=-kx\)
- Equation of motion of SHM
- Why are two initial conditions needed
- General solution:
  - \(x(t)=A_1\sin(\omega t)+B_1\cos(\omega t)\)
  - \(x(t)=A\sin(\omega t+\alpha)\)
- Physical meaning of amplitude and phase
- Determining constants from initial conditions
- Velocity and acceleration in SHM
- Relation between velocity and position
- Important extreme points in the motion

## Learning goals
By the end of this class, the student should be able to:
- distinguish periodic motion, oscillation, and SHM,
- explain the physical mechanism behind oscillation,
- derive the SHM equation of motion from Hooke’s law and Newton’s second law,
- understand why SHM needs two independent constants,
- interpret amplitude and phase physically,
- determine the motion from initial position and velocity,
- relate displacement, velocity, and acceleration in SHM,

## Key idea
In simple harmonic motion, the farther the particle is from equilibrium, the more strongly it is pulled back. That simple restoring-force law leads to sinusoidal motion.
