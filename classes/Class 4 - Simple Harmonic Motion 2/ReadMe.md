# Class 4 — Simple Harmonic Motion (Part 2)

## Overview
This class continues the discussion of simple harmonic motion by focusing on time period, frequency, phase, phase difference, numerical applications, and mechanical energy.

The class builds on the standard SHM form
\[
x(t)=A\sin(\omega t+\alpha)
\]
and explores what the constants mean physically, how to solve typical numerical problems, and how kinetic and potential energies change during the motion.

## Main topics
- Time period \(T\), frequency \(\nu\), and angular frequency \(\omega\)
- Why \(\omega = 2\pi\nu\)
- Phase and initial phase
- Phase difference between two oscillations
- Phase relation between displacement and velocity
- Numerical problems based on the SHM equation
- A useful trick: shifting the time origin
- Kinetic energy in SHM
- Potential energy in SHM
- Total mechanical energy and its conservation
- Small-angle pendulum as SHM
- Vertical spring-mass system

## Learning goals
By the end of this class, the student should be able to:
- derive the relation between \(T\), \(\nu\), and \(\omega\),
- interpret phase and phase difference,
- understand why displacement and velocity differ in phase by \(\pi/2\),
- solve standard numerical SHM problems,
- use time-origin shifting to simplify calculations,
- derive kinetic and potential energy expressions in SHM,
- understand why total mechanical energy remains constant in ideal SHM,
- connect the simple pendulum and spring-mass system to the SHM framework.


## Key idea
In SHM, the motion is not only sinusoidal in time; it also has a phase structure and an energy structure. Kinetic and potential energies keep exchanging, while the total mechanical energy remains constant in the ideal case.
